# Lonely Binary VGA 800 x 600 - engineering notes

What the board is, how it behaves, the numbers we measured, and why it runs at
the pixel clock it does. The short version is in the README; this is the long
one, for people who write sketches for it or who want to know what is going on
underneath.

```cpp
#include <LonelyBinaryDisplay.h>
LB_Display display(LB_VGA_800X600);
```

## 1. What it is

An ESP32-S3 (N16R8: 16 MB flash, 8 MB octal PSRAM) and a VGA output board. 16
GPIO lines run through a 16-bit resistor ladder (5 red, 6 green, 5 blue) into
the three analog colour signals of a DE-15 connector. There is no display
controller and no screen memory on the board: the ESP32-S3 sends every pixel of
every frame, continuously, from a framebuffer in PSRAM.

| | |
|---|---|
| Picture | 800 x 600, RGB565 (65,536 colours) |
| Timing | 32 MHz pixel clock, 1024 x 625 total, positive sync: **50 Hz** |
| Framebuffer | 960 KB in PSRAM, plus a second one of the same size (about 2 MB of the 8 MB) |
| Pins | HSYNC GPIO 2, VSYNC GPIO 1, R0..R4 = 7, 15, 16, 38, 39, G0..G5 = 17, 18, 8, 3, 9, 10, B0..B4 = 11, 12, 13, 14, 21 |
| Button | BOOT (GPIO 0) is the only one |
| Serial | UART0 on GPIO 43 / 44 (through the USB-serial chip), not native USB |
| Not available | touch, rotation (0 only), MicroPython |

Arduino IDE settings: **ESP32S3 Dev Module**, **PSRAM: OPI PSRAM**, **USB CDC On
Boot: Disabled**, Partition Scheme **Huge APP** for the large examples.

## 2. How the picture gets to the monitor

1. Your sketch draws into the framebuffer in PSRAM (through the CPU's data
   cache). `display.flush()` writes the cache back so the new pixels are really
   in PSRAM.
2. The ESP32-S3's LCD_CAM peripheral sends 16 data lines plus HSYNC and VSYNC
   to the board. A VGA monitor has no data-enable and takes no pixel clock, so
   the peripheral runs in "HV mode": the sync pulses alone frame the picture.
3. The peripheral does not read PSRAM directly. Two **bounce buffers** in
   internal RAM, 10 lines (16,000 bytes) each, take turns: while one is being
   sent, an interrupt **copies the next 10 lines from PSRAM into the other one
   with the CPU**. That copy has a hard deadline: 10 line times.

Everything below follows from point 3.

## 3. The numbers

### 3.1 The refill budget

| Pixel clock | Line time | 10-line budget | Data per budget | Rate needed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 36 MHz (VESA 800x600 @ 56 Hz) | 28.4 us | **284 us** | 16,000 B | 56 MB/s |
| 32 MHz (what ships) | 32.0 us | **320 us** | 16,000 B | 50 MB/s |

### 3.2 What the copy actually costs

Measured on the board with the picture running (16,000 bytes, PSRAM to internal
RAM, CPU copy):

| Data | Time |
|---|---|
| Not in the data cache ("cold"), best case | **250 - 300 us** |
| In the data cache (recently drawn) | 163 - 173 us |

The data cache is 32 KB, 8-way, 32-byte lines. A cold copy therefore uses
essentially the whole 284 us budget at 36 MHz. There is **no margin**: whether
a given group of lines arrives in time depends on what the cache happens to
hold, which depends on what the sketch drew last and where its code sits in
flash.

### 3.3 What that looks like

At 36 MHz, with the same code:

- Short horizontal streaks at the **left** of some lines (about the first 200
  pixels), repeating text rows from about 20 lines above - that is stale data
  from two bounce buffers ago.
- On some sketches the top rows jitter when the sketch updates the screen.
- The streaks are there even when the picture is **static**.
- Changing one unrelated string in the sketch (which shifts the code in flash)
  turned the streaks on or off, identically on every reset.
- `scanStats()` reported 0 late frames the whole time: it measures the length
  of a frame, not of a line, so it cannot see this.

At **32 MHz** the same binaries are clean. That is why the board ships at 32
MHz and 50 Hz: not the VESA standard, but a monitor locks to it without
trouble, and the picture keeps the margin.

### 3.4 Drawing speed

The scan-out uses most of the PSRAM bandwidth, so writing into the screen is
slow:

| Operation | Time |
|---|---|
| Full-screen fill | about 0.23 s |
| 40 x 40 rectangle | about 0.2 - 0.3 ms |
| Full-screen JPEG decode | about 1.3 s (half size: 0.6 s) |
| `flush()` with nothing new | about 16 us |
| Throughput | 2 - 5 million pixels a second |

One frame is 20 ms, so a loop that animates at the full 50 Hz must draw well
under 100,000 pixels per frame.

## 4. Writing sketches for it

1. **Draw what changed, never wipe and redraw.** Clearing a region and drawing
   it again takes tens of milliseconds, the monitor reads the screen while you
   do it, and for a moment it shows the region empty. It looks like blinking.
   Instead: draw the fixed parts once; for text use `setTextColor(fg, bg)` so a
   new number lands on the old one with no gap, padded to a fixed width; for
   lines, take the old ones off by drawing them again in the background colour,
   then draw the new ones. `Vga07_Clock`, `Vga08_Chart` and `Vga11_Dashboard`
   do exactly this.
2. **Call `flush()`** after drawing.
3. **Keep Wi-Fi and Bluetooth off while the picture matters.** The radio and the
   screen share the PSRAM bus and the picture shakes while the radio is on.
   Turn it on, fetch what you need, turn it off.
4. **Do not do big PSRAM reads or writes** (large `memcpy`, a big JPEG) while an
   animation runs.
5. **Keep important things about 24 pixels from the top and 16 from the sides.**
   Monitors crop and offset a non-standard picture differently; the LVGL
   example did lose the top of its title until it was given a margin.
6. **Never name a constant `PS`, `OK`, `WARN` or `CRIT`.** ESP32 headers define
   them as macros and the error points somewhere unrelated.
7. **Custom types in an `.ino` belong in a `.h`.** The Arduino build inserts
   function prototypes above your `struct`/`enum`.
8. **Verify with your eyes.** The frame counter and `late frames` stay healthy
   while a line-level problem is visible on the screen.

## 5. Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| No picture, `begin()` returns false | PSRAM not enabled | Tools > PSRAM > OPI PSRAM |
| Nothing on Serial Monitor | USB CDC On Boot is Enabled | Set it to Disabled, use the UART port |
| Picture cut off or off-centre | Monitor locked to the mode off-centre | Monitor's AUTO button |
| Wrong colour in one bar or a ramp with a step | A data line is open or shorted | Run `Vga02_ColorBars`: the 16 patches name the line |
| Picture shakes while Wi-Fi is on | Radio and screen share the PSRAM bus | Turn the radio off while it matters |
| Blinking in a region you update | Wiping and redrawing it | Section 4, rule 1 |
| Streaks at the left of some lines | Refill margin (section 3). Only if the pixel clock is raised above 32 MHz | Keep the default clock |
| Last letters of a line cut off | The text is wider than 800 px | Measure with `textWidth()` |

## 6. How to read the monitor's behaviour

- The 50 Hz picture is not a VESA preset; monitors measure the sync and show
  what they measure. If a particular monitor crops or stretches it, the AUTO
  button is the first thing to try. If one does not lock at all, tell us the
  model.
- The black of the picture often looks blue-grey on a phone camera. That is
  the camera and the monitor's black level, not the board.

## 7. What was tried and ruled out

For anyone who wants to improve the timing later.

| Tried | Result |
|---|---|
| CPU load while the picture runs: reading flash, reading PSRAM, writing PSRAM, `memcpy` of 1 MB, drawing text | No disturbance at 32 MHz. (Not the cause.) |
| Position of the refill interrupt | In internal RAM, same address in good and bad builds. (Not the cause.) |
| No bounce buffers (scan straight out of PSRAM) at 36 MHz | Worse. |
| Data-cache auto-load (hardware prefetch) through the ROM | The ROM reports "Dcache preload parameter configuration error" and the chip panics; the interface is undocumented. Not usable. |
| Pixel clock 32 MHz, nothing else changed | **Clean.** This is the fix. |

Not tried, if more resolution or colour at higher clocks is wanted one day: an
intermediate clock (34 / 35 MHz) to find the real threshold; larger bounce
buffers; filling the bounce buffers ourselves from a more compact framebuffer
(the older Lonely Binary VGA library does that at 640 x 480 with 16 colours);
a custom Arduino core with 64-byte data-cache lines or PSRAM at 120 MHz.
